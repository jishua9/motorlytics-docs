The G-G diagram is available on **Pro and Team** plans and requires a file with lateral (`gLat`) and/or longitudinal (`gLon`) G-force channels.

## Accessing the G-G diagram

1. Open a session with telemetry imported.
2. Select the **Track Map** tab.
3. Click the **G-force** button in the map toolbar (the activity icon). The G-G diagram appears as an overlay panel in the corner of the map.
4. Click the button again to hide it.

The visibility preference is saved — if you leave the session and return, the diagram stays visible or hidden as you left it.

## Reading the diagram

The G-G diagram is a scatter plot with one axis for lateral G and one for longitudinal G. The convention used in Motorlytics:

| Direction on the plot | Meaning |
|-----------------------|---------|
| Top | Braking (deceleration) |
| Bottom | Acceleration (throttle) |
| Left | Cornering left |
| Right | Cornering right |

Each data point is a single GPS sample from the selected laps. Points are rendered at 40% opacity so density — how much time the car spends in a particular region — is visible.

A **stats overlay** in the top-right corner of the diagram shows:
- **Max Lat** — peak lateral G reached across all selected laps.
- **Max Lon** — peak longitudinal G reached across all selected laps.

Values are in G rounded to two decimal places (e.g. `Max Lat: 1.84G`).

### Lap colours

Each lap is plotted in its own colour, matching the colours used in the lap list and charts:

| Position in selection | Colour |
|-----------------------|--------|
| First lap | Blue |
| Second lap | Red |
| Third lap | Green |
| Fourth lap | Amber |
| Fifth lap | Purple |

### Live position marker

While the Track Map playback is active, a **solid dot with a white border** moves across the G-G diagram to show the current G-force loading at the playback position. The marker colour matches the lap it belongs to.

## What to look for

- **Full use of the traction circle** — points spread evenly to the extremes in all four quadrants indicate the driver is consistently reaching the limit of grip.
- **Trail braking** — points in the top-left and top-right quadrants (combined braking and lateral load) indicate the driver is carrying brake into the corner.
- **Understeer** — low lateral G relative to longitudinal G on corner entry suggests front-end push.
- **Oversteer** — high lateral G with sharp drops in longitudinal G on corner exit can indicate rear instability.
