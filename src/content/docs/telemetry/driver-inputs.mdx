Driver input analysis is included with your Motorlytics subscription. The throttle, brake, and steering channels must be present in the uploaded file. Check the **Data Channels** card on the **Laps** tab to confirm.

## Accessing driver input traces

Driver input channels (Throttle, Brake, Steering) are available in two places:

### Charts tab

1. Open a session with telemetry imported and select the **Charts** tab.
2. The channel selector pills at the top show only the channels present in your data. Click **Throttle**, **Brake**, or **Steering** to add them to the chart.
3. Up to three channels can be active simultaneously. Selecting a fourth channel drops the oldest selection (FIFO order).
4. Use the **Overlay** / **Stacked** toggle to either combine all channels on one chart or separate them into individual panels.

Multiple laps can be loaded via the **Floating Lap Selector** panel. Each lap is coloured separately (blue, red, green, amber, purple) so you can compare input patterns between laps directly.

### Track Map data panel

1. Select the **Track Map** tab.
2. Click the **chart panel** button in the map toolbar to open the data panel.
3. Select **Throttle**, **Brake**, or **Steering** from the tab bar inside the panel.

The data panel syncs with the map playback position. As the playback marker moves around the track, a vertical reference line moves through the input chart to show the current position.

With Throttle and Brake selected in Stacked mode and two laps loaded, you get two panels stacked vertically, a Throttle panel on top and a Brake panel below, each with one coloured trace per lap running left to right across the same Lap Distance axis.

**What to look for in the throttle panel**: a clean trace rises sharply from 0% to 100% at corner exit and holds flat until the next braking point. If two laps' throttle traces rise at different points along the axis, that is where one driver got back on the power earlier than the other. A trace that hovers in the middle of the range rather than snapping to 0% or 100% shows partial throttle, sometimes deliberate (modulating on a slippery or off-camber exit) and sometimes a sign of hesitation.

**What to look for in the brake panel**: compare the leading edge of each brake trace to spot the braking point, the earlier the rise, the earlier the driver braked. A steep near-vertical rise is a hard, confident brake application. A trace that tapers off gradually rather than dropping straight to 0% shows trail braking, carrying some brake pressure into the corner as steering input increases.

**Reading the two traces together**: overlay where the brake trace falls to 0% against where the throttle trace starts to rise. If there is a gap between the two, meaning the car is neither braking nor accelerating, that is time coasting through the corner with no input, usually a sign of hesitation or an overly cautious line. Traces where brake release and throttle application happen almost back-to-back, with little or no coasting gap, generally belong to the faster lap. Comparing two laps stacked this way makes braking-point and throttle-application differences visible even before you check the resulting lap time delta.

## Channel details

### Throttle (THR)

- **Range:** 0 to 100%
- A flat line at 100% is full throttle. The shape of the throttle-on point after a corner exit reveals how aggressively the driver is applying power.

### Brake (BRK)

- **Range:** 0 to 100% (normalized) or raw PSI if the data is recorded in brake pressure
- When the raw brake pressure exceeds 100 (indicating PSI data), Motorlytics normalizes it to a 0 to 100% scale relative to the session peak. The axis label reflects the actual unit.
- Steep leading edges indicate hard initial brake applications; a tapered release shows trail braking into the corner.

### Steering (STR)

- **Range:** −90° to +90°
- Positive values are right-hand steering input; negative values are left-hand. A centre reference line at 0° is shown in the chart.
- Smooth, early steering inputs typically indicate a better line. Corrections (rapid oscillations) can indicate instability or late turn-in.

## Lap Distance axis

The horizontal axis in all driver input charts is **Lap Distance**: a normalized 0 to 500 point index that maps proportionally across the full lap. Absolute distance in metres is not shown on the axis, but hovering over the chart shows the value for each channel at that point.
